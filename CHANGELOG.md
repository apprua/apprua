# Changelog

Every release of Apprua, newest first. Each section is also the text of the release on GitHub.
The version numbers follow [semantic versioning](https://semver.org): only a new major version (2.0) can change how things work; minor and patch versions (1.1, 1.0.1) keep it.

## [1.0.0]

**English**

The first public release, after a full security review. Updating is recommended for every installation.

- New installations work again: since 1.0.0-rc.1, installing Apprua on a PC or a server where it had never been stopped with no message at "Connecting Apprua to Coolify" (updates were not affected).
- Windows: "Uninstall" in Settings > Apps keeps its window open when something goes wrong, so the message can be read, writes what it shows to `%TEMP%\apprua-uninstall.log`, and is no longer stopped by a notice of WSL. The installer stops with an explanation when it is started from inside an app that keeps the files it writes in a private copy (some apps from the Microsoft Store, for example an AI app that runs commands for you): there the icons, the start at sign-in and "Uninstall" would not work. It says to open it from a normal PowerShell window instead.
- A final review, of 1.0.0-rc.4, found more, now fixed: a Dockerfile could still bring labels for the proxy by hiding them in a continued line (a comment, a space or an empty line inside it, or a word split in two); the check now reads the lines the way Docker does. A server copy, also the nightly one, could stop with no message when the engine knew the volume of an app that Docker had never created (an app whose first build failed). The installer no longer suggests removing everything without a copy when it finds another first user in Coolify, starts a Coolify that is there but stopped instead of installing over it, and keeps working when the administrator of Apprua's own Coolify changed their email. On a minimal Debian server the installer adds iptables first, so the engine's panel is closed before the engine exists. A full copy is refused on a server whose Coolify also holds other projects. A paused app is no longer started by "restart" (it said paused while running). The "forgot your password?" email no longer goes in copy to the administrator, and very long emails are refused there too. After a restart the guard sees every app at once, no longer up to a minute later.
- Licenses: the connector carries the licenses of the libraries inside it (at the end of its file, and in `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.txt` in the npm package); the npm package has its README and license; the Claude Desktop extension and the downloads of the portal carry the license; `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` lists the open source work Apprua includes (libraries, fonts, icons). The release archives hold all the pages the README and the guides link to.
- Texts: three commands in the guides had lost their backslashes; publishing takes a few minutes, not a few seconds; the icon next to the clock is described as it is; SECURITY.md says how version 1.0.0 of the connector is published and what Apprua contacts outside the machine; the README says that apps published with Apprua do not fall under its license; TRADEMARKS.md tells the brand from the pictures that ship with the code; the contributor agreement is no longer marked as a draft.
- Privacy: a new page of the guide for whoever runs Apprua for other people says what Apprua keeps, where, for how long, who can see it and what leaves the machine. A new command removes an account when the person asks (`sudo bash install/remove-account.sh <email>`): the account goes and its email in the history is replaced by a random label. The request log of the portal is kept to at most 5 files of 10 MB.
- The contributor agreement follows the structure of the Apache individual agreement, gives the right to relicense contributions only to the maintainer (everyone else receives them under the AGPL), has a version number tied to the signatures, and can pass to a successor of the project. TRADEMARKS.md no longer adds limits to the AGPL on the pictures: its rules cover only the name and logo used as a brand.
- The sign-in in front of the apps no longer shares the portal's session with the apps. Before, on a local network or on the internet, an app could read the session of the people who opened it and act as them on the portal. Now the portal's cookie stays on the portal, and each app gets its own cookie, valid only for itself. Everyone signs in once more after the update.
- An app can no longer bring its own instructions for the proxy (labels in the Dockerfile, also from the image it starts from), which could take the portal's address.
- The engine's panel is closed on IPv6 too, and from before the engine is installed (an internet server was open to the first visitor for a few minutes).
- Apps run without raw network access, cannot reach the cloud metadata service, and the engine's builds cannot open the apps directly.
- AI apps install the connector from npm at the exact version of the server, no longer from the server. The connector refuses folders that are not a project (the home folder, a drive, folders of keys) and never sends the PC's keys and credentials.
- Sign-in: guessing passwords is limited, and many sign-ins at once no longer slow down the apps. The portal cannot be shown inside another page. A new password also ends any "forgot your password?" link.
- Server copies have a fingerprint checked before a restore, a restore takes back only Apprua's folders, and the nightly copy runs from a script only root can change. After every copy (also the nightly one) the lanes of the apps and the lock of the engine panel start again with Docker: before, they stayed off until the server restarted. Removing Apprua from a server where Coolify was there before leaves Coolify and its projects.
- On a local network without HTTPS the portal says that the key travels unencrypted.
- Libraries updated (nodemailer, zod); the GitHub actions of the releases are pinned to exact versions.
- A second review of these changes fixed: the lock of the engine panel always installed (also when iptables arrives with Docker, or IPv6 is off) and put back after a firewall reload; a stranger who registered first in Coolify is refused also when the installer is run again; a cookie planted by an app cannot lock people out of the other apps; very long emails refused at sign-in; removing Apprua from a server with its own Coolify cleans the proxy files; apps published before this version are not removed by the guard; the gate code only goes to the real scheme and port of the apps; when the connector of this version is not on npm, the portal serves it itself.
- An external review found more, now fixed: an archive over the limits stopped the whole server (now only that upload is refused); with "this machine only" on a Linux server the apps answered from the network without a sign-in (their ports are now closed to the network there); a connection an app opened in the moment before its lane existed stayed open (now it is cut); two publications of the same app at the same time could mix, and new apps published at the same time could go past the quota; archiving an app the engine could not stop marked it archived anyway (now it says so, and the sign-in closes archived apps in any case); a publication the engine did not take stayed "in progress" forever; publishing again after a restore could stop halfway (now it goes on from there at the next start, and Docker always starts again if a restore fails); copies on a server held the Docker volumes of every program on the machine (now only Apprua's); signing out left the apps opened with that session open for up to 12 hours (now signing out closes them, on every device); the connector followed a link or a junction to a folder of keys. SECURITY.md lists the known limits more fully.
- A second external review of those fixes found more, now fixed: archiving an app while it was being published let the end of that publication make it active again (archiving now waits, and an archived app never becomes active); with a Coolify shared with other projects the copies held the engine's database and the other projects (now only Apprua's part, and Docker keeps running); after a restore an app whose publication failed or ran out of time counted as done (now it is tried again at the next starts, up to three times); a code for one use handed out just before signing out still opened the app; an app already missing from the engine could not be archived.
- A third review, of 1.0.0-rc.1, found more, now fixed: a first publication whose setup failed halfway could leave the next attempt without the sign-in in front of the app (now the engine side is kept only when complete, and the labels with the sign-in are applied again before every publication); with a Coolify shared with other projects the guard also acted on their applications (now only on Apprua's); a start under way could make an app archived meanwhile active again (start, stop, archive and the restart after a change of settings now share one reservation, and a paused or archived app is no longer started by a change of its settings); a publication that ran out of time freed the app while the engine was still building it; the "forgot your password?" link was written in the request log (now hidden).
- A fourth review, of 1.0.0-rc.2, found more, now fixed: the reservation of a long publication could still end while the engine was building (on lost contact, after one more hour, or at a restart of Apprua); now a publication the engine has taken stays open in Apprua's database until the engine says how it ended, and is followed again at every start. Losing contact during a normal publication no longer marks it failed. The stop after an unconfirmed review date is recorded, and its email sent, only when the engine really stopped the app; otherwise it is tried again.
- A fifth review, of 1.0.0-rc.3, found one more case, now fixed: when the answer to the request to publish was lost (the engine may have started anyway), the publication was closed as failed and the app freed. Now Apprua keeps the time of the request and asks the engine whether a publication of that app started since then: if so it follows it, if the engine says no it closes it, and while the engine does not answer the app stays reserved (also across a restart).

- Una terza revisione, della 1.0.0-rc.1, ha trovato altro, ora corretto: una prima pubblicazione con la preparazione fallita a metà poteva lasciare il tentativo successivo senza il login davanti all'app (ora la parte nel motore si tiene solo se completa, e le etichette con il login si rimettono prima di ogni pubblicazione); con un Coolify condiviso con altri progetti il guardiano agiva anche sulle loro applicazioni (ora solo su quelle di Apprua); un avvio in corso poteva far tornare attiva un'app archiviata nel frattempo (avvio, arresto, archiviazione e il riavvio dopo un cambio di impostazioni ora usano lo stesso blocco, e un'app in pausa o archiviata non viene più riaccesa da un cambio di impostazioni); una pubblicazione scaduta liberava l'app mentre il motore la stava ancora costruendo; il link "password dimenticata" finiva nel registro delle richieste (ora nascosto).
- Una quarta revisione, della 1.0.0-rc.2, ha trovato altro, ora corretto: il blocco di una pubblicazione lunga poteva ancora sparire mentre il motore lavorava (perdendo il contatto, dopo un'altra ora, o a un riavvio di Apprua); ora una pubblicazione presa dal motore resta aperta nel database di Apprua finché il motore non dice com'è finita, e viene seguita di nuovo a ogni avvio. Perdere il contatto durante una pubblicazione normale non la segna più fallita. L'arresto dopo una data di revisione non confermata si registra, e la sua email parte, solo se il motore ha davvero fermato l'app; altrimenti si ritenta.
- Una quinta revisione, della 1.0.0-rc.3, ha trovato un altro caso, ora corretto: quando la risposta alla richiesta di pubblicare andava persa (il motore poteva averla avviata lo stesso), la pubblicazione veniva chiusa come fallita e l'app liberata. Ora Apprua tiene l'ora della richiesta e chiede al motore se da allora è partita una pubblicazione di quell'app: se sì la segue, se il motore dice di no la chiude, e finché il motore non risponde l'app resta bloccata (anche dopo un riavvio).

## [0.3.1]

**English**

- Codex: the portal now gives a few lines to copy into the `config.toml` file of Codex, with the key already in them, instead of a command: the Codex app has no `codex` command (on Windows it is locked inside the app). The command stays, for Codex in the terminal. Tried with the Codex app on Windows: publishing from Codex works.
- Windows with Docker Desktop: the installation no longer stops at "Pulling images". Inside the Apprua Linux, `docker` was the command of Docker Desktop (Windows programs are visible in WSL), so the engine did not install its own Docker. Now the Apprua Linux does not see the programs of Windows.
- A publication right after the PC starts no longer says "failed" while the app is going online: the engine, while it starts, marks the publications in progress as failed with no log, then completes them; Apprua now waits.
- Windows installer: the start-at-sign-in task is also created when the account name is not the usual one (for example when installing over SSH); the installation ends without an error when there is no browser to open; the copy made before uninstalling shows the right file name (it had a Windows message in front); the messages of uninstalling and restoring are in Italian too.
- Windows without WSL: the installer no longer says "WSL installed, restart" when `wsl --install` did nothing (seen on Windows 11 25H2). It turns on the two Windows features itself, asks for the restart, and on the next run installs the WSL program from the official Microsoft package (signature checked) when Windows did not. Tried on a PC without WSL: two runs and one restart, then Apprua is ready.
- Server on the local network or on the internet: "Apprua is ready" is printed only once the portal answers at `apprua.<domain>` (the proxy shows it 30-40 seconds after Apprua starts; meanwhile the address said "No app here").
- Copies on the server: no more "socket ignored" message during the copy (those files are skipped on purpose).
- New: `install/uninstall.sh` removes Apprua from a Linux server (a copy first, then type `DELETE`): Apprua, the engine, all the apps and their data, the services and firewall rules of the installer. Docker and the copies stay. In the guide, "Remove Apprua from the server".
- Restoring a copy on a new server (or after uninstalling) now works: the engine could not open its restored database (the passwords of the new installation) and the apps were not published again (the engine's SSH key of the copy was not allowed on the server). Tried: remove, install again, restore, and everything is back.

**Italiano**

- Codex: il portale ora dà poche righe da copiare nel file `config.toml` di Codex, con la chiave già dentro, al posto di un comando: l'app Codex non ha il comando `codex` (su Windows è chiuso dentro l'app). Il comando resta, per Codex nel terminale. Provato con l'app Codex su Windows: la pubblicazione da Codex funziona.
- Windows con Docker Desktop: l'installazione non si ferma più a "Pulling images". Dentro il Linux di Apprua, `docker` era il comando di Docker Desktop (in WSL si vedono i programmi di Windows), quindi il motore non installava il suo Docker. Ora il Linux di Apprua non vede i programmi di Windows.
- Una pubblicazione appena acceso il PC non dice più "fallita" mentre l'app va online: il motore, mentre si avvia, segna come fallite senza log le pubblicazioni in corso e poi le completa; ora Apprua aspetta.
- Installer di Windows: l'attività di avvio all'accesso si crea anche quando il nome dell'account non è quello solito (per esempio installando via SSH); l'installazione finisce senza errore quando non c'è un browser da aprire; la copia fatta prima di disinstallare mostra il nome giusto del file (aveva davanti un messaggio di Windows); i messaggi di disinstallazione e ripristino sono anche in italiano.
- Windows senza WSL: l'installer non dice più "WSL installato, riavvia" quando `wsl --install` non ha fatto niente (visto su Windows 11 25H2). Accende da solo le due funzioni di Windows, chiede il riavvio e al giro dopo installa il programma WSL dal pacchetto ufficiale di Microsoft (firma controllata) se Windows non l'ha fatto. Provato su un PC senza WSL: due lanci e un riavvio, poi Apprua funziona.
- Server in rete locale o su internet: "Tutto pronto" compare solo quando il portale risponde a `apprua.<dominio>` (il proxy lo mostra 30-40 secondi dopo l'avvio di Apprua; nel frattempo l'indirizzo diceva "No app here").
- Copie sul server: niente più messaggio "socket ignored" durante la copia (quei file si saltano apposta).
- Nuovo: `install/uninstall.sh` toglie Apprua da un server Linux (prima una copia, poi si scrive `ELIMINA`): Apprua, il motore, tutte le app e i loro dati, i servizi e le regole del firewall dell'installazione. Docker e le copie restano. Nella guida, "Togliere Apprua dal server".
- Il ripristino di una copia su un server nuovo (o dopo una disinstallazione) ora funziona: il motore non riusciva ad aprire il suo database ripristinato (le password erano quelle della nuova installazione) e le app non venivano ripubblicate (la chiave SSH del motore della copia non era autorizzata sul server). Provato: togli, reinstalla, ripristina, e torna tutto.

## [0.2.0]

**English**

- Your account: change your own password; every other device signed in with the old one has to sign in again.
- Forgot your password: a one-hour link by email when Apprua can send emails; otherwise an administrator gives a new password from Settings, or the administrator runs `install-apprua.ps1 -ResetPassword` (Windows) or `install/reset-password.sh` (Linux).
- After updating, everyone signs in once more (sessions are now tied to the password).
- Windows: Apprua appears in Settings > Apps, with Uninstall (the same safe removal, a copy first); the installer speaks Italian on an Italian Windows (and Linux), shows its steps ("Step 2 of 5") and ends with a "Good to know" box (where the apps live, what deletes them, copies, how to remove Apprua). The guide has the same section.
